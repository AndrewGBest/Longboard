# Code of conduct

Longboard is a small project. This is how people are expected to treat each
other in its issues, pull requests and discussions.

## What is expected

- Be direct and be kind. Criticise the work, not the person.
- Assume the other person means well and may know something you do not.
- Say what you tried and what you saw. Guesses are welcome when they are
  labelled as guesses.
- Newcomers' questions get a straight answer, not a telling-off.

## What is not accepted

- Insults, harassment, or remarks about who somebody is rather than what
  they wrote.
- Publishing anybody's private details.
- Pressing for help with anything the project's rules rule out
  ([CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md)): getting round DRM, a paywall or a region limit,
  for instance. Asking once is fine; the answer is no.

## If something goes wrong

Tell the maintainer, [@AndrewGBest](https://github.com/AndrewGBest). If it
should not be said in public, use GitHub's "Report content" on the comment,
or the [private report form](https://github.com/AndrewGBest/Longboard/security/advisories/new)
and say it is about conduct.

The maintainer may edit or remove comments, close threads, and block
accounts that keep at it. This is one person's spare-time project: there is
no committee and no appeals process, only the promise to be fair and to say
why.
